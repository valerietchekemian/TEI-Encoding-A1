TEI Encoding of A1 Written Productions

Valérie Tchekemian

Le projet

This project applies TEI (Text Encoding Initiative) markup to two short written productions at A1 level (CEFR), collected as part of a French as a Foreign Language (FFL) learner corpus. The aim is to make visible and quantifiable what a teacher often notices intuitively while marking a piece of writing. An error stops being a simple note in the margin: it becomes a classified data point, comparable across texts and analyzable for patterns.

Considérations sur cette forme d'aide à l'évaluation

Ce que ça apporte

It makes visible and quantifiable what a teacher often notices intuitively while marking a piece of writing. An error stops being a simple note in the margin: it becomes a classified data point, comparable across texts. The error taxonomy reveals patterns: interferences from the learner's first language, syntactic calques, recurring gaps. This systematization saves time in counting and provides a traceable, exploitable record for both teaching practice and research.

Ce que ça ne remplace pas

The teacher's pedagogical judgment: the error taxonomy was built from reading only two texts, and is deliberately provisional — it will need refining with a larger corpus. The tool flags patterns; it does not prescribe remediation. Nor does encoding automate error detection; the classification remains a human judgment call, made once and then recorded. With only 14 errors across 2 texts, no claim of generalizability can be made.

Ce que ça change dans la pratique

It shifts part of the correction work upstream: instead of treating each text in isolation, one can query a set of encoded texts to inform lesson planning — targeting a session on the most frequent error types, or designing grammar practice around the patterns observed. With a larger corpus, the same encoding scheme could track progression over time or across a cohort of learners.
